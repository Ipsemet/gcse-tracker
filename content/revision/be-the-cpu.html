The eight sentences

Step 01 · 5 min

Nobody loses marks here for not knowing what a MAR is. They lose them for writing the question back, or giving a fact without its effect. These are the wordings that score. The tariff tells you how many separate points to make.

State the purpose of the CPU.[1]
It processes instructions and data. Not “the brain”. Not “calculations”. And it does not store anything — storing is the job of memory.
Describe the fetch stage.[2]
The address in the program counter is copied into the MAR. The instruction at that address is copied from memory into the MDR, and the program counter is incremented.
Describe the decode stage.[1]
The control unit works out what the instruction means — what operation is wanted and what data it needs.
Describe the execute stage.[1]
The instruction is carried out. If it is arithmetic or a comparison, the ALU does it and the result goes to the accumulator.
Explain the stored program concept.[2]
Program instructions and the data they use are held in the same main memory, and are fetched and executed one at a time rather than the machine being hard-wired. Stopping at “stored together” gets one of the two.
What holds an address, what holds data?[4]
PC — address of the next instruction. MAR — address about to be read or written. MDR — the actual instruction or data. ACC — the ALU’s result. The middle word of MAR and MDR is the whole answer.
Explain how cache improves performance.[2]
It holds frequently used instructions and data close to the CPU, and is faster to reach than RAM, so the CPU waits less and executes more instructions per second. “Explain” always wants the effect.
Why does the cycle never stop?[3]
A program is a long list of instructions and only one can be processed at a time, so the cycle repeats. Even when the machine looks idle the operating system is still issuing instructions, so it only stops at power off.
